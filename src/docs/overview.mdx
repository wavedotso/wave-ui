# Color

Color in Wave communicates status, expresses the brand, and guides attention. Every color is
_semantic_ — defined by its job, adaptive to light and dark, and built to stay legible. Here's
how the palette is designed, and how to use it well.

## Use the token, never the value

**Avoid hard-coding a color.** Documented hex values are for reference only — the real values
shift with theme and appearance. Reach for a semantic token — `bg-primary`, `text-muted`,
`border-line` — and let the system resolve it. Re-skin the entire library by overriding the
semantic layer; the components never change.

**Use a color consistently.** One color, one meaning. For example, Wave Blue means
"primary / interactive," so don't reuse it to style text that isn't.

## One brand color, kept constant

Wave's brand is **Wave Blue** — a calm, trustable cobalt, chosen to be _ownable_ rather than
the blue every app already ships. It drives `--primary`, the focus ring, links (`--info`), and
the bright signal (`--accent`). It stays the **same in light and dark** on purpose: a constant
color is a constant brand.

## Work in light, dark, and increased contrast

**Supply a light and a dark variant for anything colored.** Surfaces, text and borders flip
between the light theme (Paper) and the two dark themes (Graphite · Ink) through the theme's
tokens, never a `dark:` variant. Status colors keep their palette's 500 as a fill in every
theme, with white text on it; as words and icons they take their `-text` step, the 600 on
Paper and the 400 on Graphite and Ink, so they read on each theme's own surfaces. Graphite is the one
exception: each status is a touch lighter than its 400 there, so it reads on Graphite's mid-tone menus.

## Put color in fills and symbols — not in text

The rule that shapes the whole status system: **vivid colors belong on fills, icons and dots;
text stays neutral.** Success is a green checkmark beside plain words — not green words.
Coloring text forces you to darken it for contrast until a yellow turns to brown, destroying the
very identity the color exists to carry. Keep the color in the glyph and the label neutral, and
you get identity _and_ legibility at no cost.

## Never rely on color alone

**Pair color with shape and text.** A status is an icon (eg. check, triangle) _and_ a word so
meaning survives color blindness and cultural differences (a rising stock reads green in the
US, red in China). Color reinforces meaning; it never carries it alone.

## The status palette

Four semantic roles. Calm where they can be, loud where they must be:

- **Success** — Wave Green, clearly apart from the warning orange.
- **Warning** — Wave Orange, held well away from the green.
- **Danger** — Wave Red, the most saturated of the set; an error is the one thing that should
  genuinely alarm.
- **Info** — Wave Blue. Informational _is_ the brand.

## Authored in OKLCH, wide-gamut ready

Every color is defined in **OKLCH**, so lightness is perceptually even and adjusting one channel
doesn't skew the others. On Display-P3 screens the palette renders in wide gamut; on sRGB it
falls back cleanly.

## Accessibility is the floor

**Meet contrast, and respect the person's settings.** Text targets WCAG AA (4.5:1); never
override someone's chosen appearance or contrast level. The structure tokens (`--line`,
`--edge`, `--contour`, `--highlighted`, `--neutral`, `--focus`) and the layered surfaces are tuned to keep text and
controls legible on every theme.
